# Platform image decoders behind an asynchronous prefetch

Issue #372: platform image decoders are much faster than our managed DCT decoder where it matters most. For the 8-megapixel catalogue-class photograph, Chrome's WebCodecs `ImageDecoder` takes 15.5 ms off the page's thread, against 72 ms (WebAssembly AOT) and 364 ms (interpreter) on it; SkiaSharp takes 14.9 ms and ImageIO 18.2 ms, against 48.7 ms on the JIT (`docs/research/image-decode-scaled-and-native.md`, §5). There was no good place to plug them in. A native `IImageFilter` registered with `PdfOptions.UseFilter` is synchronous, while the browser's decoders are asynchronous and the core must never block in the browser (ADR 0010). It replaces the exact managed codec for every caller, including text extraction, export and `PdfImage.Decode`, which need the exact §8.9.3 samples. And each platform package would re-implement eligibility and fallback. Native decoders are also not faithful by default: they apply Exif orientation, convert embedded ICC profiles (WebKit ignores `colorSpaceConversion: 'none'`), convert CMYK with their own inversion assumption, apply YCbCr where `ColorTransform 0` forbids it, and some cannot decode 12-bit or arithmetic-coded data.

We add a new extension point, `IPlatformImageDecoder` (`Name`, `Fidelity`, `Features`, `CanDecode`, `DecodeAsync`), registered with `PdfOptions.UsePlatformImageDecoder`, with the same asynchronous-boundary pattern as ADR 0010. `DisplayList.PrefetchImagesAsync(deviceTransform, region, samplesPerPixel, token)` replays the list into a collecting backend, finds the images kept encoded (ADR 0014) that the region reaches, works out the reduction the drawing backend will ask for (the same `DisplayImage.GetReduction` rule), and offers each to the first decoder that takes it. What comes back goes into the document's image cache under the image's per-reduction once-gate, where the replay finds it. Replay stays synchronous: a miss decodes managed and never waits on the platform, so a host with one thread cannot deadlock. The viewer awaits the prefetch before it replays a tile: `ITileBackend.PrefetchImagesAsync`, a default interface method that does nothing, which the software tile backend implements at its own reduction; the tile scheduler continues the tile in a later frame when the prefetch does not complete at once, and background and remote renders await it before they draw.

The core owns three things a platform cannot narrow away. First, one eligibility rule over the JPEG markers before the first scan and the image dictionary: DCTDecode, 8-bit, 1 or 3 components, baseline or progressive Huffman (arithmetic only for a decoder that declares it), no lossless or hierarchical frames, the frame's size equal to the dictionary's and a height in the header (no DNL), and three components only when both ISO 32000-2 Table 13 and a JFIF decoder read them as YCbCr. A decoder's `CanDecode` can only narrow it. Second, byte hygiene: the payloads of APP1 Exif and APP2 `ICC_PROFILE` segments are zero-filled in a copy for decoders that declare they apply orientation or convert profiles, keeping every offset. Third, fallback: a decoder that returns nothing, throws or delivers other samples than asked for leaves the image to the managed codec, and the document records `ImageDecodedManaged` (Information) once for that image, with the reason; the image is not offered again. Concurrent prefetches of one image at one reduction share one decode; cancellation keeps nothing.

`PdfOptions.VerifyPlatformImageDecoder` decodes both ways and records `PlatformImageVerified` or `PlatformImageMismatch` with the largest difference, the PSNR and the share of pixels more than 2 levels apart, against the decoder's declared fidelity: `Exact` allows no difference; `Tolerance` allows at most 24 levels, at least 50 dB and at most 1% of pixels over 2 levels at full size, and at least 55 dB against the managed decode at the same reduction (the research report's §6.4).

Owner decision (2026-10-19): platform decoders are on by default, including ones that are not bit-exact, within those tolerances. A platform package registers its decoder in the engines it creates; `WithPlatformImageDecoding(false)` (or `UseManagedImageDecoding`) turns them all off, and `WithPlatformImageDecoder(name, false)` turns one off.

`IImageFilter` stays the exact managed contract for every caller that is not drawing. `PdfImage.Decode`, text extraction and export always decode managed.

## Consequences

- Two paths can produce one image: native for drawing, managed for extraction. They may differ within the declared fidelity; the verification mode measures it, and the platform test suites run with it on.
- A tile whose images go to a platform waits for them before its first paint. The progressive-zoom placeholder covers the wait, and render-ahead tiles prefetch too.
- Encoded bytes of asynchronous decodes in flight stay alive until the decode ends; the tile scheduler's concurrency bounds them.
- Without a registered decoder, the prefetch returns a completed task and allocates nothing; with one, a prefetch whose images are already cached allocates nothing either (`ImagePrefetchBenchmarks`).
- Images inside soft masks and tiling-pattern cells are not offered; they decode managed as before.
- A backend may also hand an image to the platform itself and draw the platform's own image instead of samples ("mode B"): `DisplayImage.TryGetPlatformData` gives it the eligible, cleaned data. The browser's WebGL backend does this for Safari (#373), only where our colour pipeline would be the identity for that image.

## Considered Options

- Register a native `IImageFilter`: synchronous, so it cannot serve the browser; replaces the exact codec for every caller; leaves eligibility and fallback to each package.
- Make decoding asynchronous all the way down: every backend's replay would become asynchronous, and the browser would still need a boundary before its synchronous rasterizer.
- Let each viewer shell call its platform decoder itself: eligibility, byte hygiene, fallback diagnostics and cache keying would be repeated per shell, and the rule would drift between platforms.
