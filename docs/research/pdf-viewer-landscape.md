# PDF Viewer Landscape: What Broadside Must Match and Beat

*Research memo, October 2026. Sources are linked inline; forum and vendor claims are flagged as such. Web content was treated as data, not instructions.*

### OSS .NET options

- **PdfPig** (Apache-2.0): parsing and text extraction, PDFBox port; rendering only via the alpha `PdfPig.Rendering.Skia` (SkiaSharp) ([NuGet](https://www.nuget.org/packages/PdfPig.Rendering.Skia/0.1.9.1-alpha004)).
- **PDFsharp 6.x** (MIT): create/modify, cross-platform, no rasterization of existing pages ([pdfsharp.net](https://pdfsharp.net/Licensing.ashx?HL=license)).
- **PdfiumViewer**: archived 2019, last release 2017; Windows-only forks (`PdfiumViewer.Net.WPF`) survive on `bblanchon.PDFium` binaries ([NuGet](https://www-0.nuget.org/packages/PdfiumViewer.Net.WPF)).
- **Docnet**: .NET Standard 2.0 PDFium wrapper for win/linux/osx, bundled PDFium is old ([repo](https://git.tdem.in/GowenGit/docnet)).
- **QuestPDF**: generation only; Community License v3 (July 2026) is source-available, not OSI, <$1M revenue ([QuestPDF](https://questpdf.com/license/community.html)).
- Emerging pure-managed renderers: **Melville.Pdf** (WPF/Skia targets, FreeType via SharpFont) ([repo](https://github.com/DrJohnMelville/Pdf)); **Lxman.PdfLibrary 2.0** (MIT) claims managed fonts, JPEG/JPX/JBIG2/CCITT codecs and a geometry-only `IRenderTarget`, with WPF the only in-box raster target ([dev.to](https://dev.to/michael_jordan_87eaf96f24/render-a-pdf-to-an-image-in-net-with-a-pure-c-pdf-engine-a7o)).

## 2. What people love and hate

**Speed and bloat.** PCWorld on Reader: it "performs sluggishly, and uses enough resources that it can slow a whole system" while Sumatra "loads in an instant" ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/494824/pdf-7.html)). On Adobe's forum in 2025: "Documents load very slowly, if at all" and "the more pdf's you have open the slower it gets" ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-reader-discussions/adobe-slow/m-p/15096570)). Preview was once called "the fastest PDF reader on any platform" ([O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/show-me-mac/0789730669/0789730669_art01lev2sec15.html)). Lesson: perceived speed, not feature count, drives loyalty.

**Scroll smoothness and large files.** A Zotero (pdf.js) user: "scrolling is lagging heavily for the large PDF file" on Mac but smooth on Windows ([Zotero](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/478733)); an HN commenter said a large math textbook "will lockup FF" while Acrobat or Chrome scrolls it fine. pdf.js's own FAQ says a letter page at 96 DPI needs ~3.5 MB per canvas, so only visible pages are rendered ([FAQ](https://github.com/mozilla/pdf.js/wiki/Frequently-Asked-Questions)). Syncfusion and Telerik threads trace slowness to vector-heavy pages and image decoding: Telerik says a single large image "could use about 60 MB" ([Telerik](https://www.telerik.com/forums/memory-issues)); a Syncfusion user saw ~27 MB per page while printing a 657-page file ([Syncfusion](https://www.syncfusion.com/feedback/24973/print-large-pdf-document-error)).

**Zoom quality.** The universal complaint is blur during a pinch: "text became sharp again once fingers were lifted"; the vendor's explanation was that the page "is re-rendered only when the zoom operation completes" ([Syncfusion forum](https://www.syncfusion.com/forums/158178/xamarin-forms-android-pdf-texts-blurry-while-pinch-zooming-pdfviewer)). pdf.js historically fell back to CSS scaling, which "causes zoomed-in PDFs to look blurry" until a visible-region re-render patch ([PR 12212](https://github.com/mozilla/pdf.js/pull/12212)). Apple's PDFKit slows "dramatically" during zoom with many annotations ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/802728)). Chrome's pinch maps to browser zoom and pixelates while its +/- buttons stay crisp ([Opera forum](https://forums.opera.com/post/18333)).

**Text selection.** Multi-column selection is a decade-old sore point: Okular's selection "ignored column boundaries" ([KDE 276580](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=276580)); Poppler text "can't be selected in the correct order," which breaks Orca ([Poppler #277](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/poppler/poppler/issues/277)); PDF-XChange admits selection "follows the content order rather than the visual reading order" ([forum](https://forum.pdf-xchange.com/viewtopic.php?p=171048)). Preview is praised for selecting within the correct column ([Zotero](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/89925/build-in-pdf-reader-two-columns-making-trouble)) but mocked because "it thinks every number is a phone number" ([AlternativeTo](https://www.alternativeto.net/software/preview/about/)).

**Search.** Acrobat users report a 900-page book "searching page by page even with an embedded index" ([Adobe](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-reader-discussions/advanced-search-is-slow-even-embedded-index/m-p/13822081)). Syncfusion support says its search "renders each page and looks for the text in the rendered page" ([Syncfusion](https://www.syncfusion.com/forums/177960/pdfviewer-search-run-slow)); Telerik's regex search "becomes slow on large files" ([Telerik](https://feedback.telerik.com/kendo-jquery-ui/1456276-searching-large-pdf-files-causes-page-to-freeze)); Zotero's "Highlight all" was "greatly slowing searches" ([Zotero](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/491132)).

**Annotations.** Sumatra users called highlighting "the main thing missing" for years ([forum](https://forum.sumatrapdfreader.org/t/historic-older-smx-annotation-highlight-support/67)); Okular is "probably the best linux pdf viewer" but its "annotation system needs a rewrite" ([KDE 353677](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=353677)); in Preview, "upwards of half" of new pop-up notes vanished after reopening in High Sierra ([Zotero](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/291237)). Chrome had no annotation tools until 2026. Edge is praised for inking (v80) and read-aloud (v84) ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/deployedge/microsoft-edge-pdf)).

**Forms.** XFA forms show "Please wait" in Chromium viewers; Edge called the omission "by design. XFA is not part of the PDF standard" ([Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/edgeinsiderdiscussions/opening-xfa-compliant-pdf-documents-does-not-work-with-built-in-pdf-player-of-mi/3410714)). Preview "is known to corrupt such PDF files in some cases" per a former Adobe scientist ([Adobe](https://community.adobe.com/questions-9/check-boxes-not-working-on-pdf-in-mac-preview-1236279)).

**Dark mode.** "'dark mode' and 'invert colors' are NOT the same" ([Sumatra discussion](https://github.com/sumatrapdfreader/sumatrapdf/discussions/3432)); a medical reader wants "Smart Invert" that keeps radiographs intact ([Obsidian](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/pdf-smart-invert-dont-invert-pictures-while-in-dark-mode/67492)); Acrobat mobile users saw PDFs "appearing as negatives" after it adopted the device theme ([Adobe](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-reader-mobile-discussions/colors-inverting-on-their-own-cannot-seem-to-resolve/td-p/12090625)).

**Accessibility.** A May 2025 Reader update silently "altered" default accessibility settings so NVDA/JAWS stopped reading ([Adobe](https://community.adobe.com/questions-12/screen-reader-compatibility-issues-with-adobe-reader-after-latest-update-1506686)). PowerMapper rated Firefox poor in 2020 because pdf.js "didn't yet support tagged PDFs" ([PowerMapper](https://www.powermapper.com/blog/at-tests-jan-2020-update/)); pdf.js added a struct-tree layer in 2022. Google says 12% of web PDFs are inaccessible and now OCRs them with generated landmarks ([ChromeOS](https://chromeos.dev/en/posts/frictionless-pdf-accessibility-with-ml-and-ocr)). Acrobat's Liquid Mode reflow stops at 200 pages ([Adobe help](https://helpx.adobe.com/acrobat/mobile/view-manage-files/liquid-mode.html)).

**Tabs, memory, startup.** Acrobat's tab mode is a CPU workaround for some ([Adobe](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/acrobat-very-slow-to-start-up-and-very-laggy-when-using-it-on-windows-11/m-p/13977583/highlight/true)); Sumatra users have wanted tab drag-out since issue #18 ([forum](https://forum.sumatrapdfreader.org/t/moving-tabs-about/2895)). pdf.js used "a combined 457 MB of RAM" for five 1 MB files ([Mozilla support](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/questions/1169825)); Preview once needed "nearly 2 GB" for a 33 MB OCRed scan ([DEVONthink](https://discourse.devontechnologies.com/t/problems-opening-ocred-pdf-in-preview-2-gb-ram-needed/11858)).

**Mobile gestures and page modes.** Acrobat's tablet app caps pinch-out at "Fit to Page Width" in continuous mode, so "the pinch does nothing" ([Adobe](https://community.adobe.com/questions-15/zooming-out-in-acrobat-tablet-app-1638158)); Firefox disabled double-tap zoom because "Chrome and Safari either disable this or handle it badly" ([Bugzilla](https://bugzil.la/1700064)). Sumatra users complain "I can only view up to 2 pages at a time" and that scrollbars only exist in continuous mode ([UserJot](https://sumatrapdf.userjot.com/board/p/add-thumbnail-view-or-wrapping-scroll-view-in-display-mode)).

**Outline and navigation.** Edge Chromium launched without bookmarks ("no such option available") ([Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/edgeinsiderdiscussions/add-table-of-contents--bookmarks--document-outline-in-pdf-viewer/843301)); Chrome added a TOC/thumbnail side panel in Chrome 87 ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/google/heres-how-to-enable-google-chromes-new-modern-pdf-reader)); Preview hides outlines on some files ([Apple](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/2203325)).

## 3. Rendering-layer requirements implied by great viewers

1. **Tiled rendering.** Apple's reference viewer draws `CGPDFPage` into a `CATiledLayer` with 512 px tiles and levels of detail for zoom ([Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/samplecode/ZoomingPDFViewer/Listings/ZoomingPDFViewerSwift_ZoomingPDFViewer_TiledPDFView_swift.html)); an open-source reader on that pattern handled "PDF files over 250MB and over 2800 pages" ([Reader](https://github.com/lazyprogram/Reader)). PSPDFKit for Windows 2.2 renders "multiple pages or parts of a single page" in parallel ([Nutrient](https://www.nutrient.io/blog/pspdfkit-windows-2-2/)). Tiles bound memory at high zoom and make pinch-zoom sharp without re-rendering whole pages; they also fix the "blur until fingers lift" complaint. Broadside needs a `RenderRegion(page, clipRect, transform)` primitive, not just `RenderPage`.

2. **Progressive rendering with time budgets.** Chrome's engine caps a paint slice at `kMaxProgressivePaintTime = 300 ms` (250 ms for a page's first paint) and resumes via `ContinuePaint`, painting placeholders for unavailable pages ([pdfium_engine.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/pdf/pdfium/pdfium_engine.cc)). PDFium exposes this as `FPDF_RenderPageBitmap_Start/Continue` with a pause callback ([fpdf_progressive.h](https://pdfium.googlesource.com/pdfium/+/refs/heads/chromium/6797/public/fpdf_progressive.h)). The rasterizer must be re-entrant and interruptible so the UI thread is never blocked.

3. **Render-ahead and cancellation.** Chrome calls `CancelPaints()` on every scroll and resize and closes abandoned renders with `FPDF_RenderPage_Close`, "otherwise painting will never finish" ([pdfium_engine.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/pdf/pdfium/pdfium_engine.cc)). Syncfusion documents pre-fetching pages "just ahead of the scroll position" ([Syncfusion](https://www.syncfusion.com/blogs/post/smooth-scrolling-performance-image-heavy-pdfs)). Implication: every render API takes a `CancellationToken`, and a scheduler prioritizes visible tiles over look-ahead.

4. **Display-list caching across zoom.** MuPDF's `fz_display_list` lets a page be "replayed later, possibly with different transforms or clip rectangles," is thread-safe because replay "never touches the source file," at the cost of memory ([MuPDF Explored](https://ghostscript.com/~robin/mupdf_explored/mupdf_exploredse48.html)); Lxman renders to a retained WPF `DrawingGroup` for the same reason. Interpreting content streams once and re-rasterizing at any scale is what makes zoom feel instant; it also enables bounding-box culling, which a 2010 MuPDF bug showed "sped up rendering small rectangles at high zoom" ([Ghostscript bug 691756](https://bugs.ghostscript.com/show_bug.cgi?id=691756)).

5. **GPU acceleration (optional, composited).** Almost every mainstream viewer rasterizes on CPU and composites on GPU. DevExpress offers GDI+/DirectX/DirectX-software modes ([DevExpress](https://docs.devexpress.com/WindowsForms/DevExpress.XtraPdfViewer.PdfViewerRenderingEngine)); PDFium's Skia backend is still flag-gated with "different anti-aliasing and font face rendering" from AGG ([PDFium](https://pdfium.googlesource.com/pdfium/+/a05e59ab03e01f7b9e298feb92c455d6e25cba7f%5E%21/)); Windows.Data.Pdf recommends `RenderPageToSurface` for zoom above 400% ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/pt-br/uwp/api/windows.data.pdf)). Broadside's backend abstraction (software, Skia, CoreGraphics, Direct2D, Android Canvas) matches this model; pixel parity between backends must be tested, since Chrome needed separate checksums per backend.

6. **Text hit-testing and column-aware selection.** pdf.js builds a DOM text layer positioned against the viewport and links it to a struct-tree layer via `aria-owns` ([GSoC write-up](https://blog.learningequality.org/google-summer-of-code-2022-improve-accessibility-of-pdf-renderer-alex-velez-b87abf46a355)). The text layer must expose glyph quads, reading-order blocks and column segmentation; raw content-stream order is what breaks Okular, Poppler and PDF-XChange today.

7. **Page thumbnails.** Chrome 87's side panel, Okular's sidebar and Apryse's `SaveViewerOptimized` (pre-embedded thumbnails) ([Apryse](https://docs.apryse.com/web/guides/best-practices)) show thumbnails are expected; the renderer needs cheap low-DPI renders that share the display list with the main view.

8. **Background text indexing.** Viewers that search by rendering pages (Syncfusion) or ignore embedded indexes (Acrobat) are the ones users complain about. Extract text per page on a worker, build an incremental index, and support "highlight all" without re-walking pages.

9. **Annotation overlays separate from content.** pdf.js keeps `AnnotationLayer` (existing) and `AnnotationEditorLayer` (new) off the canvas ([Nutrient guide](https://www.nutrient.io/blog/pdfjs-annotation-editor-layer.md)); Okular stores annotations "separately from the unmodified PDF file" ([ServerWatch](https://www.serverwatch.com/guides/annotating-pdfs-with-the-open-source-software-package-okular/)); PDFKit, which bakes annotations into the page render, is the one that "becomes very slow" with many of them. The rendering layer should render page content and annotation appearance streams as independent, individually cacheable layers.

10. **Incremental loading over HTTP.** pdf.js "may automatically start using HTTP Range Requests" and offers `disableAutoFetch` to fetch only what the view needs ([FAQ](https://github.com/mozilla/pdf.js/wiki/Frequently-Asked-Questions), [api.d.ts](https://app.unpkg.com/pdfjs-dist@4.1.392/files/types/src/display/api.d.ts)); Apryse's Downloader streams linearized files and requires `Accept-Ranges: bytes` ([Apryse](https://docs.apryse.com/web/guides/usedownloader-option)); Nutrient asks for ~128 KB first to render page one ([Nutrient](https://www.nutrient.io/guides/web/performance/linearized-downloads.md)). The parser needs a random-access byte source abstraction (`Stream` or `IRandomAccessSource`) with hint-table awareness, which also matters for Blazor WASM.

11. **Accessibility tree and reflow.** pdf.js's struct tree, Chrome's OCR-generated landmarks and Acrobat's Liquid Mode all depend on the renderer exposing tagged structure (or inferring it) alongside geometry. This should be a first-class output of the layout pass, not a post-process.

## 4. Gaps and opportunities

- **Nothing managed-only is production-grade.** Every shipping viewer sits on C/C++ (PDFium, MuPDF, Poppler, Quartz, Adobe, Apryse, Foxit). Managed attempts are new (Lxman 2.0, Aspose.PDF.FOSS `SoftwarePageRenderer`), alpha (PdfPig.Rendering.Skia) or still bind FreeType (Melville). Native binaries bring the deployment pain developers cite: platform-specific PDFium builds ([IronSoftware](https://ironsoftware.com/suite/blog/comparison/compare-pdfium-vs-ironpdf)), `NativeFileReference` and version-pinned SkiaSharp for Blazor WASM ([Syncfusion](https://blazor.syncfusion.com/documentation/pdfviewer-2/getting-started/web-assembly-application)), and archived wrappers with no CVE patches (PdfiumViewer). A managed core with optional accelerated backends is an unoccupied position.

- **Licensing cost and opacity.** Nutrient, Apryse and Foxit are quote-only with per-app-ID or per-platform terms and add-on stacking; Aspose needs $3.5k to $16k OEM tiers for public distribution; Telerik/DevExpress start around $1k per developer per year; MuPDF is AGPL; QuestPDF just moved off MIT. An MIT/Apache rendering layer with no revenue threshold is itself a feature.

- **Inconsistent cross-platform behavior.** Syncfusion ships different engines per platform (PDFium on desktop and WASM, undocumented on MAUI); Nutrient's MAUI printing is Windows-only; Edge changed engines underneath users (XFA broke); Apple's PDFKit rewrite "slammed" third parties ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/linked/developers-report-serious-pdf-bugs-in-macos-sierra/)). Same parser, same layout engine, same display list on every platform, with backends only differing in rasterization, would give pixel-consistent output and one bug tracker.

- **Accessibility is bolted on.** Viewer-level screen-reader support regresses with updates (Acrobat 2025), was missing in pdf.js until 2022, and auto-tagging is sold as a premium add-on (Nutrient Document Engine license feature, 35 credits/page in its API ([Nutrient](https://www.nutrient.io/api/pdfua-auto-tagging-api)); Apryse requires extractable text ([Apryse](https://apryse.com/blog/auto-tagging-pdf-ua-compliance))). A renderer whose layout pass emits reading order, headings, lists and tables could offer baseline auto-tagging and reflow for free.

- **Weak layout and table extraction.** Reading order is stored separately from visual layout, so "the page can look right while its tag order is wrong" ([Adobe](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/microsoft-word-tables-print-to-pdf-layout-problems/td-p/15387614)); a CHI 2019 study extracted only 11 of 1,171 tables correctly end to end ([tablerepair](https://mail.zcliu.cs.umd.edu/tablerepair/tablerepair-CHI19.pdf)). Column detection, table inference and selection that follows visual order would fix the oldest open bugs across Okular, Poppler and Zotero.

- **Blazor WebAssembly has no .NET-native path.** Options are pdf.js interop ([SoftwareDriven.PdfJs.Blazor](https://www.nuget.org/packages/SoftwareDriven.PdfJs.Blazor)) or PDFium-in-WASM with native Skia. A managed rasterizer that runs under the .NET WASM runtime, with range-request streaming, would be unique.

**What "outcompete them all" means concretely:** one managed core (parser, fonts, codecs, layout, display list) under a permissive license; pluggable rasterizers with pixel-diff tests between them; tiled, progressive, cancellable rendering as the default API rather than an advanced mode; text, structure and annotation layers emitted as data, not pixels; range-request streaming for every platform including WASM; column-aware selection and indexed search built on the layout pass; smart dark mode that preserves images; and viewer controls that are thin shells over the same engine so MAUI, Avalonia, WPF, WinUI and Uno behave identically. The developer experience should be `dotnet add package` with zero native payload, async/`CancellationToken` throughout, and a public test corpus with golden renders.

## 5. Top 15 viewer capabilities to target (ranked)

1. **Tiled, region-based rendering with a display-list cache.** It is the single mechanism behind sharp pinch-zoom, bounded memory on 250 MB files and instant re-zoom, and every top viewer has it.
2. **Progressive, time-sliced, cancellable rendering.** Chrome's 250/300 ms slices and cancel-on-scroll are why its viewer never freezes; blocking renders are the root of the "lockup" complaints.
3. **Render-ahead scheduling with priority to the viewport.** Smooth continuous scroll on large files depends on prefetching neighbors while never starving visible tiles.
4. **Glyph-accurate text layer with reading-order and column segmentation.** Selection across columns is the most persistent cross-viewer bug, and the same data feeds search, copy and accessibility.
5. **Incremental loading via range requests and linearization hints.** First page in ~128 KB over HTTP is table stakes for web and mobile, and no managed library offers it.
6. **Separate, cacheable annotation and form layers.** Overlays rendered apart from content keep zoom fast with thousands of annotations and allow editing without re-rasterizing pages.
7. **Background full-text indexing and search with cheap highlight-all.** Users measure "slow" by search as much as by scrolling, and page-render-based search is the pattern they hate.
8. **Structure/accessibility tree output (tagged and inferred).** Screen-reader support, reflow and auto-tagging all hang off this, and competitors treat it as a paid add-on.
9. **Smart dark mode (invert text/background, preserve images).** A cheap, loudly requested feature that only PDF-XChange and Apryse do well.
10. **Thumbnails and outline/bookmark navigation from the same cache.** Expected UI since Chrome 87 and Okular; missing outlines still generate complaints about Edge and Preview.
11. **Pixel-consistent output across backends and platforms.** Developers abandon SDKs whose MAUI, WPF and Blazor builds render differently; a golden-image test suite is the proof.
12. **Form filling with AcroForm fidelity and graceful XFA handling.** "Please wait" pages and corrupted forms are the top form complaints; even detecting XFA and explaining it beats silent failure.
13. **Deterministic memory budgeting (tile eviction, image decode caps).** 60 MB-per-image and 27 MB-per-page stories come from unbounded decode; a configurable cache is the fix.
14. **Mobile gesture model: pinch, double-tap, page snapping, continuous and single-page modes.** Acrobat's fit-width cap and Firefox's disabled double-tap show how easily this goes wrong across modes.
15. **Low-overhead startup and zero native dependencies.** Sumatra's instant launch is its entire brand; a managed core that loads without extracting DLLs would carry the same advantage into every .NET host.
