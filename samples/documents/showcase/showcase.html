paged media

A short catalogue of the things HTML-to-PDF tools put into PDF files, made to give a PDF reader plenty to do: vector graphics, rounded and patterned borders, counters, footnotes, bookmarks, internal and external links, and, added afterwards by Broadside, optional content layers you can switch on and off.

Borders and backgrounds

CSS borders become stroked or filled paths in PDF. Rounded corners need Bézier curves, dashed and dotted borders need dash arrays that fit the corners, and double borders are two paths. Each box below shows one combination.

Notes, links and layers

Footnotes are numbered by a counter of their ownThis is footnote b. The counter restarts on no page; it runs through the whole document. and placed at the foot of the page by the producer. Internal links jump within the file: back to the vector graphics or the counters. External links leave it, for example to the CSS Paged Media specification or the Broadside repository.

The bookmarks panel of your reader shows this document's outline: four parts and their sections, built from the headings.

Layers

The producer of this page knows nothing about optional content, so Broadside adds it afterwards, in an incremental update that leaves the original file intact. It writes two layers: Proof marks, shown by default, which outlines the page margins on every page, and Draft stamp, hidden by default, which prints a diagonal “Draft” across each page. Open the layers panel in a reader that supports them and switch them on and off.

If your reader has no layers panel, the proof marks are simply part of the page, and the draft stamp is never shown. Both behaviours are correct.
